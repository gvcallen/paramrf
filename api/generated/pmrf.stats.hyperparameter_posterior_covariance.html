hyperparameter_posterior_covariance

pmrf.stats.hyperparameter_posterior_covariance(linearization: Linearization, evaluator: MarginalLogLikelihood, space: Space = 'declared', prior_precision: Literal['moment_matched', 'hessian'] = 'moment_matched') → Array

The linearised posterior covariance of an evaluator’s hyperparameters.

Mathematical Formulation

The linearised posterior over \([\theta; \psi]\) is block diagonal, since the \(\theta\)–\(\psi\) block of the Fisher matrix vanishes (see pmrf.objectives.evaluators.MarginalLogLikelihood.linearize()). Its hyperparameter block is

$$Sigma_{psi,text{post}} = (F_psi + Sigma_{psi,0}^{-1})^{-1},$$

with the prior precision \(\Sigma_{\psi,0}^{-1}\) taken from the evaluator’s hyperpriors by the prior_precision rule of posterior_covariance_factor(). Both terms enter through symmetric square roots with negative eigenvalues clipped to zero, so with prior_precision='hessian' a log-convex hyperprior adds none. Unlike \(F_h\), \(F_\psi\) of different fits does not add, since the hyperparameters belong to one evaluator, so this takes one linearisation.

Parameters:

  • linearization (Linearization) – The linearisation of evaluator’s fit, in space.

  • evaluator (MarginalLogLikelihood) – The evaluator linearised, carrying the hyperpriors. Must still be wrapped.

  • space ({'declared', 'physical', 'raw'}, default='declared') – The space of the parameter values. See pmrf.Param.

  • prior_precision ({'moment_matched', 'hessian'}, default='moment_matched') – How the hyperpriors’ precision is taken, as for posterior_covariance_factor().

Returns:

The covariance with shape (q, q) over the hyperparameters in psi_names order.

Return type:

jax.Array

Raises:

  • ValueError – If the linearisation is in another space, has no hyperparameters, or is over hyperparameters other than evaluator’s, or prior_precision is unknown.

  • NotImplementedError – With prior_precision='moment_matched', if space is 'raw', a hyperprior is over raw space, a hyperprior has no variance(), or a joint hyperprior is not a multivariate normal.
