ADR-0009: MAP fits are bounded least squares, alternating with a hyperparameter step

Status: accepted (2026-10)

Context

Physical line models often have parameters that are each identifiable but jointly near-degenerate: the loss is a long, narrow valley. On a synthetic MicrostripLine fit (10-500 MHz, w, h, ep_r, tand, sigma and length free, noise 1e-5, Jacobian condition number about 4e5 in box space), starting from a perturbed point:

The exact Hessian adds the residual-weighted second derivative of the model, the term a linearisation drops. Far from the solution it hurts rather than helps.

Under a Gaussian likelihood with fixed noise and discrepancy hyperparameters, the negative log posterior is half the squared norm of a residual vector, up to a constant, whenever each prior is Gaussian, truncated Gaussian or uniform. Noise and kernel hyperparameters are usually fitted too, and they enter through \(\tfrac{1}{2}\ln|\Sigma_D|\), which is not a square.

Decision

A least-squares minimiser over box space

pmrf.optimize.ScipyLeastSquares wraps scipy.optimize.least_squares with 'trf'. Like any minimiser that honours bounds, it searches box space and is given the box’s edges (ADR-0007). It minimises half the squared norm of a residual vector, not a scalar.

The residual vector

The vector stacks the whitened residual of each term and the prior residual of each free parameter.

  • Whitened residual. For a MarginalLogLikelihood, \(L^{-1} r\) per event block, with \(L\) the lower Cholesky factor of \(\Sigma_D = K + \Sigma_n\): the same factor linearize uses. For a TargetLoss with MSELoss, the prediction error scaled so that half its squared norm is the loss.

  • Prior residual. Computed from the distribution, in the space it is over. It is not the raw value, since truncation makes the raw map nonlinear.

A truncation normaliser is constant in \(\theta\), so it does not move the MAP.

Alternating with a hyperparameter step

The level above the minimiser splits the free parameters. The model parameters are the free parameters of problem.model. The hyperparameters are the free parameters the terms own, such as white noise and kernel hyperparameters. run_minimizer hands the minimiser both groups: a residual function of the model parameters given the hyperparameters, and the full scalar objective of the hyperparameters given the model parameters. The minimiser knows nothing about noise or kernels.

Each round is a least-squares solve over the model parameters, then a hyperparameter step: a scalar minimisation of the full negative log posterior over the hyperparameters with the model parameters held, by hyper_minimizer (default ScipyMinimize()). The fit stops when a round no longer decreases the full objective. With no free hyperparameters it is a single least-squares solve.

Not yet supported

Each of these raises an error saying it is not yet supported:

  • noise given as a callable of the prediction, since \(\Sigma_D\) then depends on the model parameters;

  • a mixing that depends on the prediction, for the same reason;

  • orthogonal discrepancy without a fixed reference basis;

  • priors other than those in the table, such as a prior over raw space carried to declared space with a change-of-variables term;

  • GibbsMarginalLogLikelihood, losses other than MSELoss, and any term without a whitened residual.

Considered options

  • Restore the exact Hessian in ScipyMinimize. Rejected as the fix: it did not converge on the degenerate fit and is slow to compile. It may still be added for other reasons.

  • Optimistix least-squares solvers. They keep the fit in JAX, but take no bounds, and in raw space they stall on the bounds. Rejected.

  • One least-squares solve over model parameters and hyperparameters together. The log-determinant is not a square; writing it as one gives a residual with a singular derivative. Rejected.

Consequences

  • At the MAP, the stacked whitened Jacobian and prior square root of the last least-squares solve are the \(A\) of posterior_covariance_factor. The fit and its linearisation share one approximation.

  • ScipyMinimize’s commented-out Hessian path is removed.

  • Shared discrepancy is supported once its whitened residual covers the joint covariance across datasets, for mixings that do not depend on the prediction.
